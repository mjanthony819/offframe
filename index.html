    What does this picture give away?

    Photos often carry the exact spot they were taken, the phone that took them, a serial number, a timestamp and sometimes a name. Offframe lists it all in plain words, removes it, and then checks that the picture itself did not change.

    1. Look. Every finding, ranked by how much it gives away.
    2. Remove. Metadata is cut out of the file. Pixels are not re-encoded.
    3. Check. The cleaned file is re-read, hashed and decoded to prove the image is unchanged and nothing is left.

    What it will not do. It does not blur faces, signs or screens, and it cannot make a picture anonymous. See the limits at the bottom of the page.

    Limits, stated plainly

    • Pixels are untouched. A face, a street sign, a house number or a screenshot of a private page stays in the picture. Offframe only removes data stored around the image.
    • Formats: JPEG, PNG and WebP only (including animated PNG and WebP, and WebP with transparency). Anything else is rejected, not guessed at. HEIC, TIFF, RAW and video are out of scope: HEIC needs a full decoder and is refused rather than converted.
    • Some metadata is kept on purpose. The rotation flag and the colour profile (both optional), plus display chunks such as transparency, gamma and pixel density in PNG. A colour profile can contain a vendor or software name. Untick the option to drop it.
    • Recognised metadata only. It removes EXIF, XMP, IPTC, comments, thumbnails, PNG text and time chunks, multi-picture data, unknown application data, unknown WebP chunks and bytes after the image end. A format feature it does not know about is removed if it is optional, and the file is refused if it is required and unknown.
    • Not a forensic guarantee. Hidden patterns in the pixels (for example watermarks) are outside what it can see.
    • Receipts hold labels, never values. A receipt says "GPS coordinates were removed", not what they were.
    • Demo data is invented. The samples use made-up people, serials and notes. Their GPS points are public landmarks used as stand-ins.
